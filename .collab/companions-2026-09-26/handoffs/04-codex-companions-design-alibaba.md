## Framing first

**Options the brief does not list**
1. **Do not score per lineage.** The key fragments: 63 consults / 39 ratings spread over 15 lineages for 11 roster entries — Moonshot appears as `kimi :: k3`, `byteplus :: kimi-k2.5`, `byteplus :: kimi-k3`; DeepSeek under `byteplus ::` and `alibaba ::`; gemini in two tiers. That is ~0.3 ratings per (lineage, purpose) cell, so the per-purpose branch will almost always fall through. Alternatives: score per `lab` with the model as a correction, or per lineage ignoring purpose until n≥5.
2. **Replace the reward model with rotation.** Least-recently-used over eligible entries gives R14's exploration with no scores, no seed, no reproducibility problem, and it is explainable. The draw earns its complexity only once ratings are dense.
3. **Size by budget, not stakes.** Cost is quota and wall clock — endpoint groups run serially (`Get-PanelPlan`), so a purpose-keyed size can still ask for four members of one endpoint.

**To choose between them I need:** the rating rate you expect per reviewer per month; whether a panel must be auditable/reproducible or merely varied; and whether roster models stay stable for 90 days (they do not: `gpt-5.1`→`gpt-6-astra`).

## Q1
Additive is wrong for this data. A single `no` gives max(0.25, 1−1)=0.25, **below** the neutral 1.0 of a never-rated entry, so one disappointed judge benches a reviewer for 90 days while an untried one outranks it. And weights are unbounded: 30 `yes` (31) against 10 and 1 makes the "weighted draw" ≈ always pick the best. Use a rate with a prior — w = (yes + 0.5·partly + 2p)/(n + 4p), p = 0.5 — mapped onto [0.25, 2]. Keep the 90-day window but cut on the **consultation's** `when`, not the rating's: re-rating rewrites `when` (codex-findings.ps1:410).

## Q2
0.2/slot is high: at k=4, P(≥1 explored)=59%, and with ~0.3 ratings/cell the weights are noise, so you pay one random member per panel for information you do not have. Prefer 0.1 flat, or 0.2 decaying **per entry** as 0.2·5/(5+n) with n = its ratings on that purpose, so a proven reviewer stops being gambled on while a newcomer keeps being sampled. Record n in `routing` or the decay is unauditable.

## Q3
Yes — your own roster shows it (see the `lab` finding). Second regret case: the "best-ranked of each lab" is computed over the *eligible* set only after the `panel: weighty` gate (codex-consult-common.ps1:4910), so diversity can force in `gemini-3.8-flash` while excluding `gemini-3.1-pro-high`. Run the pass over eligible entries, skip it when k ≥ distinct labs, and log which rule filled each slot.

Explicit `-Effort`/`-MaxWords` already override (codex-consult.ps1:936). A role file may *recommend* values in prose, never apply them.

## Q5
Per endpoint — the data has no per-entry granularity: health keys on `provider_fingerprint` (codex-consult-common.ps1:4425); the three `byteplus` entries share one base_url, and every agy entry shares SHA-256(`cc-engine-v1|agy`) (2472). ## Verdict: ADVISE

The R14/R15/R16 framing is workable, but the design's two central mechanisms contradict each other (deterministic lab-diversity vs. seeded weighted draw), the telemetry key and the `lab` default both fragment against the live roster, and the availability addendum restates behaviour that already exists while the code that would produce its line short-circuits before it can name every entry that is out.
