**Q1. Judge.** I found no raw roster-label, host, or coordinator-string escape through the new judge serialization. `ConvertTo-TelemetryJudge` rebuilds the object from closed lists; an in-memory hostile-input probe produced only `other/other/rating_actor`.

`rating_actor` correctly takes precedence. However, it is never saved with the mark. If both spool attempts fail, backfill substitutes the consultation coordinator, even for a new 0.6.1 rating whose actor was known. **R1 / finding #1** narrows the remaining F02-3 defect. The site's weighting must also distinguish an actual rating actor from consultation provenance; the latter is not proof of who judged.

**Q2. consult_ref.** Independent GUID generation avoids deriving task or person information from the token. Consultation/rating serialization shares the token correctly, legacy entries omit it, and the complaint-details exclusion worked in my probe.

This is pseudonymous linkage, not unconditional anonymity: the ledger stores the exact token alongside task context. Sharing that ledger permits an exact join with telemetry; existing instance identifiers also remain linkable (**R6**, informational).

The identifier alone does not finish latest-rating semantics. Live ratings and retries use event-construction time rather than the committed mark's time, and timestamps lose subsecond precision. A delayed retry can appear newer than its replacement; two distinct rating times also serialized identically in my probe. **R2 / finding #2** replaces the remaining F02-4 concern.

**Q3. Reset.** Read-only Windows PowerShell 5.1 probes reproduced:

- **R3:** `9:43 PM` parsed at `21:43:00.500` becomes tomorrow: nearly 24 hours of hold from half a second of delay.
- **R4:** Berlin's repeated hour, reference `2026-10-25T02:15+01:00`, reset `2:30 AM`: returns October 26, although October 25's second 02:30 is 15 minutes away.
- **R5:** `resets at 21:43 UTC` on a +02:00 machine becomes 21:43 local, two hours early. The regex silently accepts the clock prefix and ignores its timezone.

English PM and the spaced `p. m.` variant worked. Other localized designators remain unproven. The added tests cover ordinary rollover and a nonambiguous time across DST; they explicitly bless rollover 30 seconds after a displayed minute and do not cover the repeated-hour case.
